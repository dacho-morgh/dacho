## Conflicts against approved requirements

- **Signup field contract remains unresolved by Figma.** Product requirements §4 define stage 1 as eight values: legal company name, national ID, activity domain, representative name and position, representative phone, email and password. The live signup section establishes a three-step flow and counts field instances (6/4/2), but does not expose enough field labels to validate mapping, requiredness, or whether fields are split between stage 1 and later completion. Treat the approved stage-1 list and atomic company/person creation as authoritative; document T03's validation dictionary independently.
- **At inspection time, the review rubric lacked an authoritative source.** The design explicitly assigns 45/40/15 category weights and displays score ceilings and final score examples, while the then-current requirements left these open. The later user-supplied forms independently confirm the 45/40/15 weights and exact per-review formula; use [review-rubric.md](review-rubric.md) for source questions, ceilings and open interpretations. Do not infer a 100-point normalization, pass threshold or automatic advancement from the design.
- **Reviewer count needs a mapping decision in UI contracts.** The assignment modal says “three reviewers” while the approved model is three distinct chief-assigned reviewers plus the fixed subtopic employer reviewer (four total active positions). Its four-row/4-of-4 counter may be consistent if one row represents that fixed slot, but metadata/screenshots do not establish this. FE contracts should separately represent three chief assignments, one employer slot, and four finalized current-round evaluations.
- **Profile completeness must follow requirements, not sample completeness.** The profile references show identity, contact and address data, but do not evidence every approved stage-2 field (knowledge-based status, center residency, documents, services, key members, projects/customers, etc.) or explicit “Not available / Does not have” answers. These remain requirements from product requirements §4 and T03; presence of the profile frames does not close them.
- **Application workflow is not specified by the reviewed frames.** The visible dossier/form and review UI do not establish independent application identity per subtopic, accepted consent text/version/actor/time, RFP/proposal distinction, saved draft recovery, revision-bound new review rounds, or role-based data redaction. Product requirements §§1, 3, 4 and 6 govern these contracts.
- **Historical file-limit captions are not policy.** The earlier frontend audit records a universal 12 MB caption in proposal-format references. Product requirements §5 leave per-category format/size/count open (Q39) and explicitly reject treating 12 MB as approved. No file limits should be lifted from legacy frames.

## Unresolved inputs for implementation

1. T03 must publish the exact signup/profile field dictionary, type, normalization, requiredness/conditionality, and absence semantics. Figma's field instances cannot supply that contract.
2. The user-supplied forms now provide the ordinary/employer question sets, ceilings, weights and formula. Resolve the remaining A3, qualitative requiredness, rounding and four-review aggregation questions in [review-rubric.md](review-rubric.md) before final score-form acceptance; verify Figma copy against that source.
3. Confirm how the assignment UI identifies the pre-assigned employer reviewer versus the three chief-selected reviewers, including replacement authority and current-round finalized count.
4. Approved agreement/rules text and file-category policy still need Product decisions. The target role and dossier projection contract is now in [permission-matrix.md](permission-matrix.md); implementing and testing it remain later work.

## frontend_contracts findings

- Model signup as the approved eight stage-1 values, with the company/person creation boundary and duplicate-national-ID recovery from requirements; keep the visual three-step shell only if the actual flow mapping is approved.
- Model profile completeness and verification separately from legal operating status; maintain explicit missing/unavailable values and submitted revision identity.
- Represent event application as one case per subtopic, with separate RFP/proposal references, consent evidence, resumable draft ID, active revision and round.
- Represent the review round as three assigned reviewers plus one employer reviewer, four distinct assignments and four finalized current-round reviews; expose a chief's explicit next action separately from `4/4` readiness.
- Keep rubric schema versioned and data-driven; source score math and result fields from the user-supplied forms in [review-rubric.md](review-rubric.md), with unresolved interpretation decisions explicit.
- Keep reviewer/company views server-filtered. The profile and evaluation frames visually co-locate company data and review content, which cannot grant access to financial or unrelated-case data.
